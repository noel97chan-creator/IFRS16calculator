Understanding IFRS 16 Lease Accounting

Implementation of IFRS 16 has fundamentally changed how companies account for leases. Unlike the previous standard (IAS 17), IFRS 16 eliminates the distinction between operating and finance leases for lessees. This means that nearly all leases must now be recognized on the balance sheet.

How this Calculator Works

This tool helps accountants and financial analysts quickly compute the present value of lease payments to determine the Lease Liability and the initial Right-of-Use (ROU) Asset. Here is a breakdown of the inputs:

  • Monthly Payment: The fixed amount paid to the lessor every period.
  • Lease Term: The non-cancellable period of the lease, plus any extension periods that are reasonably certain to be exercised.
  • Incremental Borrowing Rate (IBR): The rate of interest that a lessee would have to pay to borrow over a similar term, and with a similar security, the funds necessary to obtain an asset of a similar value to the right-of-use asset in a similar economic environment.

Why is the "Payment Timing" Important?

The timing of your cash flow significantly impacts the Present Value (PV) calculation:

  • Arrears (End of Month): Interest accrues before the payment is made. This is standard for most loans.
  • Advance (Start of Month): The payment is made immediately. The first payment reduces the principal directly without incurring interest for that first month.

Calculation Methodology

We utilize the standard Present Value of an Annuity formula adjusted for timing. The Right-of-Use Asset is initially measured at the amount of the lease liability (assuming no initial direct costs or lease incentives for this simplified calculator). The asset is then depreciated on a straight-line basis over the lease term.
