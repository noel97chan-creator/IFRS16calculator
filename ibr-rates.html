Current IBR Rates for IFRS 16 Lease Accounting

Last Updated: December 23, 2025 | Next Update: January 2026

This page provides current Incremental Borrowing Rate (IBR) benchmarks from official central bank sources across key markets. These rates serve as reference points for determining the discount rate when calculating lease liabilities under IFRS 16, SFRS 16, and MFRS 16.

What is IBR?

The Incremental Borrowing Rate is the rate of interest that a lessee would have to pay to borrow over a similar term, and with similar security, the funds necessary to obtain an asset of similar value to the right-of-use asset in a similar economic environment (IFRS 16, Appendix A).

How to Determine Your IBR for IFRS 16

According to IFRS 16 (Appendix A), if the rate implicit in the lease cannot be readily determined, the lessee shall use the incremental borrowing rate. Here's how to determine an appropriate IBR:

Start with Benchmark Rate

Use the relevant central bank rate or prime lending rate for your jurisdiction as a starting point:

  • Singapore: Prime Lending Rate (4.25% - 5.75%)
  • Malaysia: OPR + spread or BLR (6.40% - 6.65%)
  • Hong Kong: Best Lending Rate (5.00% - 5.25%)
  • Australia: RBA Cash Rate + spread (3.60% + margin)

Adjust for Entity-Specific Factors

Add or subtract basis points based on:

  • Credit rating: Higher credit risk = higher IBR
  • Lease term: Longer terms typically have higher rates
  • Asset type: Real estate vs equipment may have different spreads
  • Security: Secured vs unsecured financing

Consider Actual Borrowing Costs

Reference your entity's actual borrowing rates:

  • Recent loan agreements with similar terms
  • Credit facility pricing
  • Bank quotes for asset financing
  • Corporate bond yields (if applicable)

Document Your Methodology

For audit purposes, document:

  • Benchmark rate used and source
  • Adjustments made and rationale
  • Comparison to actual borrowing costs
  • Board or management approval (if required)

Professional Judgment Required

IBR determination requires professional judgment and should be reviewed by qualified accountants or auditors. The rates on this page are reference benchmarks only and must be adjusted for entity-specific circumstances. Always consult with your external auditors or accounting advisors before finalizing IBR selections for financial reporting.

Industry-Specific IBR Considerations

Real Estate Leases

Typical Range: Prime Rate + 0.5% to 1.5%

Real estate leases typically have lower IBRs due to:

  • Property as collateral reduces risk
  • Longer lease terms (5-10 years common)
  • Stable asset value

Singapore Example: DBS Prime (4.25%) + 1.00% = 5.25%

Equipment Leases

Typical Range: Prime Rate + 1.5% to 3.0%

Equipment leases often have higher IBRs due to:

  • Asset depreciation risk
  • Technology obsolescence
  • Lower resale value

Malaysia Example: BLR (6.50%) + 0.50% = 7.00%

Vehicle/Fleet Leases

Typical Range: Prime Rate + 1.0% to 2.5%

Vehicle leases fall in the middle range:

  • Moderate depreciation
  • Established resale markets
  • Shorter lease terms (3-5 years)

Hong Kong Example: HSBC BLR (5.00%) + 1.50% = 6.50%

Technology/IT Leases

Typical Range: Prime Rate + 2.0% to 4.0%

Tech leases typically have the highest IBRs:

  • Rapid technological obsolescence
  • High depreciation rates
  • Limited residual value

Australia Example: RBA Cash (3.60%) + 3.00% = 6.60%
